## Step 4: LinkedIn Post (draft)

> 🔌 **Detecting Electricity Theft with Machine Learning**
>
> Electricity theft costs utilities heavily in non-technical losses. Smart meters record daily consumption, so theft often shows up as a level drop, runs of zero readings, or sudden spikes. For my project I built a system that turns that history into a per-consumer risk score.
>
> **Methodology:** I engineered 15 features from each consumer's year of daily readings, covering level change, variability, zero readings, and spikes. I compared six approaches: Logistic Regression, Random Forest (tuned), Gradient Boosting, SVM, KNN, and an unsupervised Isolation Forest. Evaluation used stratified 5-fold cross-validation and a held-out test set, with F1 and ROC-AUC because accuracy is misleading at 15% theft prevalence.
>
> **Dataset:** 1,000 synthetic consumers × 365 days of readings, with realistic benign anomalies such as vacant homes and genuine growth so the task is not trivially easy.
>
> **Results (held-out test set):** Gradient Boosting reached F1 0.871 and ROC-AUC 0.987. Bypass theft was the hardest type to catch (73% recall), while intermittent and spike-and-drop theft were caught fully in the test set.
>
> **Takeaway:** supervised models rank theft risk very well, but thresholds must be set from inspection costs, and real utility data is the next step.
